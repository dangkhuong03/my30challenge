## 3. Baseline curriculum analysis

### 3.1 Structure and actual teaching

The order is coherent at a broad level: D1 diagnosis; D2–5 reasoning/math; D6–14 probability, inference and modelling with reviews on D7/D14; D15–21 RL and initial systems with D21 review; D22–28 workflow, serving, security, evaluation, product and agent architecture with D28 review; D29 architecture capstone; D30 final. There are reviews at least every seven sessions.

The strongest actual explanations concern Bayes base rates ([L80](LESSONS.md#L80)), prediction-time leakage ([L152](LESSONS.md#L152)), unknown external outcomes and transactional outbox ([L216](LESSONS.md#L216)), durable ownership ([L232](LESSONS.md#L232)), structural credential isolation ([L252](LESSONS.md#L252)), and evaluation floors ([L262](LESSONS.md#L262)). The answer templates demand assumptions, mechanisms, failure boundaries, units and counterarguments. These are useful and should be preserved.

Most capsules, however, consist of a short theory paragraph, a one-sentence “new example,” two task prompts and a generic checkpoint. “Derive weighted least squares” ([L54](LESSONS.md#L54)) and “solve support-agent MDP” ([L168](LESSONS.md#L168)) do not show a worked derivation or specify enough data to solve one. Learners often must invent the example, the exercise and the test of correctness. Optional external resources may help an experienced learner but do not provide an assigned, validated instruction-to-mastery path for all 100 capabilities.

### 3.2 Prerequisites, workload and feasibility

File observation: normal days are 120 minutes, four reviews 150, capstone 180, baseline/final 210, including their grading time. **23×120 + 4×150 + 180 + 2×210 = 3,960 minutes = 66 hours.** The 150-minute normal-day routing would be **77.5 hours** if other day types remain unchanged. These are planned hours, not observed completion times. The ordinary-session rhythm in [CHALLENGE68–73](CHALLENGE.md#L68) sums to 120 minutes; supplemental reading and optional diagnostics must remain inside that budget.

Evaluator inference: this can be a demanding review cycle for a learner already strong in prerequisites; it is not evidence of a novice-to-advanced pathway. The actual baseline and available time are UNKNOWN. Specific pressure points are D3–4 (linear algebra/calculus plus proofs), D6 (probability plus estimation theory), D12–13 (multiple model families plus causal/ranking theory), D17–18 (several RL estimators/algorithms), and D20/23/27 (many systems mechanisms per session). The root roadmap's longer schedule is a conflicting planning opinion, not empirical proof that 66 hours is impossible.

D6 and D13 explicitly reduce content to recognition and defer it to selective review. Selection is not a completion schedule: one of coupon/birthday/length bias plus one of order statistics/sufficiency/Fisher/LRT cannot ensure each capability is independently practiced. Similarly, one causal and one ranking extension cannot ensure cosine, cold-start, uplift, propensity/IV and BT are all reconstructed. Some of these are tested in reviews before a complete explanation is supplied.

Foundations not adequately established include covariance/conditional-expectation calculations, completeness/regularity, matrix derivative and spectrum proofs, HMM/KM recurrences, algorithm tracing, SQL isolation and HTTP/cache semantics. The plan names these subjects but contains no prerequisite checklist with individual diagnostic thresholds and correction work. LSH is introduced D5 before probability; the proof of UCB later depends on an unworked Hoeffding foundation.

### 3.3 Progression, retrieval, feedback and integration

Difficulty grows mainly through **breadth and combination**, not a documented progression from worked example to partially guided task to independent task to adversarial transfer. The same answer templates remain throughout; no explicit fading of hints or controlled increase of ambiguity is specified. Counterexamples, red teams and trade-offs are valuable but often self-selected and optional.

Retrieval is allocated ten minutes daily ([CHALLENGE68](CHALLENGE.md#L68)), with four named reviews. Concrete later prompts retrieve HMM, TD, REINFORCE, OPE, workflow and evaluation; several capabilities never receive a later explicit test. There is no per-capability due-date/retrieval ledger or delayed retention gate. Recurrence of a label is not evidence of retained mastery.

Feedback consists of checkpoints, 0–4 self-rubric, baseline/final point keys, generic weekly grading and error taxonomy. The review diagnostics request root-cause-based retries, but they are optional, often learner-authored, and [DAA87](DAILY_APPLIED_ASSESSMENTS.md#L87) explicitly permits only surface changes. A learner can therefore fail an unrecognized misconception, read a cue and repeat the same structure without a compulsory materially changed reassessment. There is no independent reviewer or calibrated grader evidence; self-grading is not automatically invalid, but these keys cannot reliably adjudicate all advanced derivations and underspecified designs.

D29/[F8](ASSESSMENTS.md#L121) integrate ownership, external effects, approval, versioning and recovery. D19→D26 links reward hacking to product Goodhart; D9→D25 links statistical noise to release decisions. These are sound integration opportunities. Mathematics, specialized statistical estimators and RL proofs receive much less capstone integration. A paper design is an observable artifact, but a design request or test plan alone does not demonstrate that a build-test-feedback-iterate loop occurred.

Two narrow technical checks supporting G09:

- **MDP baseline:** [A6](ASSESSMENTS.md#L39) defines constant rewards and transitions for retry ([ASSESSMENTS.md:39](ASSESSMENTS.md#L39)). Retry count need not be part of a Markov state in that specified model. [K48](ASSESSMENT_KEYS.md#L48) should award a *conditional* argument that real-world transitions/rewards might depend on retry history, not presume dependence from an omitted counter alone.
- **PPO:** for negative advantage, the clipped objective is flat when a sufficiently *decreased* ratio would otherwise improve it; an increased bad-action probability remains penalized by the unclipped branch. With advantage −1 and epsilon 0.2, ratio 0.5 yields −0.8; ratio 1.5 yields −1.5. [L188](LESSONS.md#L188) does not show this distinction. This audit's algebraic check follows Equation7 and Figure1 in the original [PPO paper, page3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.06347). It is an ambiguity/insufficient-instruction finding, not a claim that the entire RL lesson is incorrect.

## 10. Final conclusion

**Proven from current files:** the challenge has 30 daily contracts, broad topic organization,100 unique benchmark mappings, two actual longer worked reasoning passages, useful invariant/assumption templates, regular review prompts, meaningful systems integration opportunities and a browser payload matching its Markdown sources.

**Partially supported:** reusable reasoning in probability, causality, RL and systems; selective material transfer; advanced design prompts; evidence-oriented Done criteria; recovery and reflection. These are existing design strengths, not observed learner competence.

**Fails the governing objective:** seven required mechanisms have no adequate taught-to-tested path; near-direct rehearsal is credited as transfer; evaluator authority conflicts; baseline domains are misallocated; final exposure, limited sampling and weak evidence gates cannot establish independent coverage of all 100 questions and unfamiliar advanced variants.

**UNKNOWN:** the learner's starting level, actual effort and completion, correctness of any work outside these files, demonstrated transfer, retained mastery, final performance and unique grading conventions for ambiguous benchmark prompts.

**Curriculum remediation is required before this challenge can credibly meet the complete Day 30 objective.** Preserve the sound structure and target the gaps above. Completing or clicking through the existing 30 days, even with an 80% sampled final score, is insufficient evidence for the requested universal, transferable competence.
